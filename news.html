Research September 2026

Presenting Our Work on the Future of Software Engineering at the ACM AI Leadership Summit

HERO Labs participated in the ACM AI Leadership Summit in Atlanta, an event that brought together AI leaders and experts from ACM Special Interest Groups around the world to discuss the current state and future direction of artificial intelligence.

A highlight of the summit was presenting our work with Zixuan (Steve) Feng: “You Can’t Open an LLM With a Screwdriver”: The De-Democratization of Software.

The paper examines an important tension in AI-assisted software development: artificial intelligence may democratize access to software creation without necessarily democratizing control. More people can now generate code, but responsibly inspecting, evaluating, integrating, maintaining, and governing AI-generated software still requires substantial software engineering expertise.

Our argument is that AI is not eliminating software engineering expertise. Instead, it is changing where that expertise matters. Software engineers are increasingly responsible for specifying intent, orchestrating AI behavior, evaluating generated outputs, integrating artifacts into larger systems, and maintaining accountability for the software that is ultimately deployed.

The work also explores tensions that may shape the next phase of software engineering, including productivity versus burnout, abstraction versus fundamental knowledge, democratization versus dependency, and automation versus accountability.

The summit reinforced a central idea behind HERO Labs: software engineering has an important role to play in translating increasingly powerful AI technologies into systems that are responsible, reliable, efficient, and designed to benefit people.

Community May 2026

Invited to the Copenhagen Symposium on Human-Centered AI in Software Engineering

HERO Labs Principal Investigator Italo Santos was invited to participate in the 3rd Copenhagen Symposium on Human-Centered AI Adoption in Software Engineering, held at Aalborg University Copenhagen.

The invitation-only symposium brought together researchers and practitioners from the software engineering community for three days of intensive discussion and collaborative work on how artificial intelligence is reshaping software engineering research and practice.

Rather than following a traditional conference format built around paper presentations, the symposium used collaborative activities and Liberating Structures to create space for participants to develop research directions, challenge assumptions, and work together on shared questions.

Discussions explored topics including community guidelines for LLM use in software engineering research, AI adoption within software teams, developer mental models of agents, cognitive debt, human judgment in AI-assisted decision making, executable science, and the changing career pathways of software engineers.

The symposium also continued work on the Copenhagen Manifesto and on research workstreams designed to extend collaboration beyond the three-day event. Participating in the symposium provided an opportunity to contribute HERO Labs' perspective on human-centered AI, sustainable AI-assisted software engineering, and the evolving relationship between people and intelligent software tools.

Community April 2026

Helping Organize the Student Mentoring Workshop at ICSE 2026

HERO Labs Principal Investigator Italo Santos served as a Co-chair of the Student Mentoring Workshop (SMeW) at the 48th IEEE/ACM International Conference on Software Engineering (ICSE 2026).

The workshop brings together students and experienced members of the software engineering community to help support the next generation of researchers. It is designed particularly for undergraduate and master's students considering Ph.D. programs, as well as early-career Ph.D. students beginning their research journeys.

The one-day program combined discussions about research careers and effective mentoring with a keynote, small-group mentoring pods, networking activities, and a panel discussion with members of the software engineering research community.

Italo co-organized the workshop alongside Victoria Jackson, Daniel Russo, Cleyton Magalhães, and Adriana Sejfia. The experience reflects HERO Labs' broader commitment to mentorship, research education, and creating pathways for students to participate in the software engineering research community.

Research November 2025

HERO Labs at ASE 2025 and AIware 2025 in Seoul

HERO Labs participated in the 40th IEEE/ACM International Conference on Automated Software Engineering (ASE 2025) and the 2nd ACM International Conference on AI-powered Software (AIware 2025) in Seoul, South Korea.

At AIware, Italo Santos presented “Model-Assisted and Human-Guided: Perceptions and Practices of Software Professionals Using LLMs for Coding” , a collaborative study with Cleyton Magalhães and Ronnie de Souza Santos.

The study draws on a global survey of software professionals to understand how developers are integrating large language models into real-world coding workflows. Participants described benefits including increased productivity, reduced cognitive load, and faster learning, while also identifying challenges such as inaccurate outputs, limited context awareness, and ethical risks.

The findings suggest that software professionals generally view LLMs as assistive tools rather than autonomous replacements, highlighting the continuing importance of human judgment in AI-assisted software development.
